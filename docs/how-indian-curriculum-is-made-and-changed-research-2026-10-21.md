# How Indian School Curriculum Is Made and Changed

Research compiled 2026-10-21. Every claim lists its source. "Primary" means a government, board or parliamentary document. Items marked VERIFY were not confirmed from a primary source in this pass.

## 1. The chain, top to bottom

| Layer | Who | What they decide | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy | Ministry of Education | National Education Policy 2020: 5+3+3+4 stages, three-language principle, reduced content load, examinations in Grades 3, 5 and 8 | NEP 2020 (education.gov.in), PRS summary |
| Framework | National Steering Committee (chaired by K. Kasturirangan) with a Mandate Group | National Curriculum Frameworks: NCF-FS 2022 (ages 3-8) and NCF-SE 2023 (school). Sets curricular goals, competencies, learning standards per stage | Rajya Sabha UQ 177, 24.07.2024 (primary); NCF-SE 2023 document (dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/update/ncf_2023.pdf, primary) |
| Syllabus and textbooks | NCERT through the National Syllabus and Teaching-Learning Material Committee (NSTC), a high-powered committee empowered to develop syllabus and teaching-learning material for Grades 3-12 | Turns NCF goals into syllabi, textbooks, teacher handbooks; covers NCF-SE subjects and emerging subjects as needed | NCERT notice on re-constitution of NSTC (ncert.nic.in/pdf/announcement/Re_constitution_High_Powered_Committee_NSTC.pdf, primary) |
| Subject teams | Curricular Area Groups (13 reported) and Textbook Development Teams per subject and grade | Draft each book through mapping of curricular goals, competencies and learning outcomes | NCERT notice above (TDTs, primary); Careers360 report for the count of 13 CAGs (secondary, VERIFY) |
| Oversight | National Curriculum Frameworks Oversight Committee (notified 21 July 2023) | Checks that materials faithfully translate the NCF | NCERT NSTC notice (primary) |
| Board adoption (CBSE) | Subject Committees of Courses, then the Curriculum Committee, then the Examination Committee, then the Governing Body | Committees of Courses lay down the syllabus per subject and recommend textbooks; the Curriculum Committee recommends curricula and syllabus for Board exams; the Examination Committee sets paper duration and marks after Committee of Courses input; all committee recommendations go to the Governing Body for approval | cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/committees.html and cbse.gov.in organisational set-up page (primary) |
| Board release (CBSE) | Academics unit | Publishes the annual curriculum for Classes IX-XII (released 1-2 April 2026 for 2026-27) and issues circulars for mid-year changes | CBSE Circular Acad-14/2026 (primary) |
| Board (CISCE) | Research Development and Consultancy Division (RDCD) | Curriculum development and review, preparation of ICSE and ISC syllabi, syllabi for new subjects; publishes Regulations and Syllabuses per examination year | cisce.org RDCD page (primary) |
| State boards | Each State Board, usually with its SCERT | Preparation or adoption of curriculum is the State Board's responsibility; NCF is a common core with flexible components | Lok Sabha answer on agriculture in school curriculum (eparlib.nic.in, primary, older) |
| Open schooling | NIOS | Own courses at OBE A/B/C, Secondary, Senior Secondary | NIOS pages (primary). Internal approval body VERIFY |

## 2. How they decide what goes in

1. **Policy goals first.** NEP 2020 sets the intent (for example reducing content load, experiential learning, multilingualism).
2. **Mass consultation.** NCF-SE drew inputs from about 16 lakh stakeholders through face-to-face and digital consultation; the pre-draft went out for public comment on 6 April 2023. (Rajya Sabha UQ 177, NCF-SE 2023)
3. **Stage-wise competencies.** The NCF defines curricular goals and competencies per stage; syllabi and books are mapped to them, then to learning outcomes. (NCERT NSTC notice; Careers360 report)
4. **Expert drafting and review.** Textbook Development Teams draft; review workshops finalise (textbook front matter lists the development committee and review workshop members). (NCERT Class IX Science 2022 reprint front matter)
5. **Board fit.** Boards then decide scheme of studies, weightage, internal assessment and paper pattern through their own committees. (CBSE committees page)

## 3. How they decide what changes

| Trigger | Real example | Source |
|---|---|---|
| New framework | NCF-SE 2023 led to new NCERT books rolled out grade by grade (Grades 1-9 by 2026-27) | NCERT announcements; Careers360 report |
| Load or disruption | 2022 "rationalisation" after COVID. Official criteria: overlap with other subjects in the same class; overlap with lower or higher classes in the same subject; difficulty level; content easily accessible through self or peer learning; content not relevant or outdated; learning outcomes already developed across classes | Lok Sabha UQ 156, 18.07.2022 (primary) |
| Policy on languages | CBSE made three languages (R1, R2, R3) compulsory in Class IX from 1 July 2026 via circular in mid-May, with batch-wise transition rules | CBSE Acad-33/2026, Acad-49/2026 (primary) |
| Exam reform | Board-level changes in scheme of studies released with each year's curriculum | CBSE Acad-14/2026 (primary) |

## 4. What the current system does badly (and what an AI system must not copy)

| Weakness | Evidence | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| Slow cycle | Frameworks are years apart; textbooks follow grade by grade; Class 9 Part-II books arrived in September 2026, mid-session | Continuous source watch; versioned curriculum per session |
| Piecemeal edits break coherence | Critics argued removing one period from a history sequence left the narrative lopsided (The Wire, secondary); periodic table removed from Class 10 but kept in Class 11 (NCERT clarification via Careers360, secondary) | Automated coherence checks on the skill graph before any change ships |
| Changes not always announced | NCERT reportedly said "minor changes need not be notified" (The Week, secondary) | Every change, however small, goes in a public, dated changelog with reason and source |
| One pace for all | Same syllabus and calendar per grade | Per-learner pacing on a shared skill graph; grade is a label, mastery is the truth |
| Weak feedback loop | Outcome data rarely flows back into textbook design within a cycle | Learning-gain data feeds content improvement every day, within guardrails |

## 5. Lifelong learning and professions: the official spine

| Item | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| National Credit Framework (NCrF) | One meta-framework joining school (NSEQF), higher education (NHEQF) and skills (NSQF). 1 credit = 30 notional learning hours; 40 credits = 1,200 hours a year. Credit levels: school up to 4, higher education 4.5 to 8, vocational 1 to 8 | UGC Report on NCrF (ugc.gov.in/pdfnews/9028476_Report-of-National-Credit-Framework.pdf, primary, VERIFY figures against it); summaries by IMPRI and Drishti (secondary) |
| Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) | Digital store where earned credits are deposited, transferred and redeemed | Same |
| APAAR | Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry, conceptualised under NDEAR | education.gov.in NCrF and APAAR session note (primary) |
| NSQF and NCVET | Skills qualification levels and the regulator that adopted NCrF (12 May 2023, per IMPRI, VERIFY) | IMPRI (secondary) |
